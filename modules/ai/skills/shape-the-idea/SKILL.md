# Shaping an idea

The user has an unfinished idea, or only a feeling that something is wrong. Your job is to make it concrete enough that
the user can decide what they want.

Don't change anything (files, commits, messages) until the user tells you to start, and don't ask whether you should
start.

## 1. Find the principle

Read everything the idea touches and everything related to it, then work out the principle behind the idea.

This is the most important step. The user's points are often parts of a single idea that they haven't named yet, and
that idea is usually what they are looking for. Find what the points have in common, and check that the principle
explains every one of them. Then use it to find the parts of the current state that disagree with it.

If you aren't sure, offer the possible principles rather than making one up.

## 2. Present it

Cover the following, in this order, and skip any item for which you have nothing important to say:

1. The principle, and how each of the user's points fits it.
2. The problems it reveals in the current state.
3. The directions. If there is more than one, recommend one.
4. A draft of what the result could look like.
5. The effects that the user didn't mention, such as a rule that the idea conflicts with.
6. The open questions, each with your recommendation.

## 3. Continue the discussion

- Update the draft to match each reply. If only a small part changes, show only that part.
- Check that the principle still explains every point, including the new ones.
- If the user doesn't answer a question, go with your recommendation and say so.
- If the user is right, say so. Don't defend a point just because you made it.
- After each change, look for parts of the draft that are no longer needed.

## 4. Start the work

When the user tells you to start, carry out the last draft that the user saw, and nothing more.
